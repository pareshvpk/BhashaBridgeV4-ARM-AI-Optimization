§3What actually drives latency

Cost tracks decoder steps, not input words. Across the ladder each additional output token costs about 47 ms; the encoder and tokenizer contribute a roughly fixed floor of ~26 ms. Note w05 (5 words) and w04 (4 words) both emit 5 tokens and land within 6 ms of each other, while w07 jumps to 11 tokens and nearly doubles.

The 18-token ceiling. Translator.maxLength = 18 caps the greedy loop, and both the 15-word and 18-word sentences hit it exactly — which is why their medians (862 ms and 867 ms) are nearly identical despite the longer input. Past ~14 words the app stops translating more, it just truncates. That cap, not the CPU, is the real limit on long sentences.

§4Decode throughput

Tokens per second over the whole translate call. Throughput rises with sentence length — from 18.1 to 22.8 tok/s — because the fixed encoder and tokenizer cost amortises across more decoder steps. It stays within a narrow band, which is the signature of a no-cache decoder: every step redoes the whole prefix, so per-token cost never drops the way a KV-cache run's would.
